## Why Fitness Professionals Recommend Intake Of Soya Chunks

### Best budget protein source

At roughly ₹0.80-1.20 per 10g of protein, soya outperforms most animal proteins on cost efficiency, which matters if you’re tracking intake on a tight budget.

### Complete protein

It is a complete protein which means it has all nine amino acids that the body can not make on its own. It is one of the few vegetarian foods that can completely substitute animal protein in a meal.

### High in fibre

Soya is a good source of fibre, containing about 13g of fibre per 100g dry weight. This will slow down digestion and keep you feeling full longer between meals.

### Naturally low in fat

The low cholesterol content makes it a good option for managing cardiovascular risks, along with a fat loss goal.Long shelf life

Dry soya chunks can be kept for months without refrigeration, unlike paneer or raw chicken, so you don’t have to make a daily trip to the grocery store to keep up with your intake.

### Fits into daily meals

It works in curries, salads, stir-fries, and even savory breakfast dishes, which makes it easier to hit a daily protein target without repeating the same meal.

The results still depend on how you use the soya itself. The portion size and overall diet balance matter more than the food itself.

## Soya Chunks Vs Tofu

Tofu and soya chunks are both fantastic plant-based protein sources, but they act differently in your body and in your kitchen. Tofu is coagulated soy milk pressed into soft blocks. It’s lower in calories, easier to digest and perfect for people with a sensitive stomach or people who are counting calories. But soya chunks are just dehydrated textured soybean protein, more dense, chewy and much higher in protein per serving. A 100g serving of soya chunks has about 52g of protein while tofu has just 8-10g of protein. If you want to grow muscle or if you want to recover after an exercise, Soya chunks are definitely your best bet. For a better choice for Hormones, go for tofu. Easier digestion and less calories than soya chunks. But both are complete proteins, meaning they have all the essential amino acids not found in plant meals.
