When the Party Door Knocks, It Usually Wants Your Soul
Abhijeet Dipke's mother reports a 'join or else' ultimatum from the BJP. We look at the absurdity of political recruitment in India.

Satire · Independent commentary
Independent editorial satire on this website — not an official Cockroach Janta Party movement statement. Read disclaimer.

“Politics in this country has become a high-stakes game of musical chairs where the chairs are rigged and the music is a constant, droning threat.”

The latest news cycle has gifted us a scene that feels less like a functioning democracy and more like a low-budget mafia flick. According to reports from The Economic Times, the mother of Cockroach Janta Party founder Abhijeet Dipke has alleged that her son was issued a classic 'join us or face the consequences' ultimatum by local BJP representatives. It is the kind of interaction we have all come to expect: the professional art of political arm-twisting performed with the grace of a sledgehammer.
The Art of the Unsolicited Membership Drive
There is a special kind of audacity required to walk up to someone and suggest that their entire ideological existence is negotiable. When you are building a movement that thrives on the biting truth of how broken the system is, the last thing you want is a recruitment call from the very people you are critiquing. Yet, here we are. The incident, as described by Dipke’s family, highlights the exhaustion of Indian political life where individual agency is treated as a temporary glitch that needs to be patched by a party membership card.If you are not with us, you are against us, and if you are against us, you are apparently an existential threat that needs to be coerced into compliance.
Why the Cockroach Still Bites
The CJP was never intended to be a comfortable place for status-quo maintainers. Our existence is predicated on the fact that the current machinery is rusted, loud, and fundamentally uninterested in the people it claims to serve. When power structures feel the need to threaten a movement, they are not showing strength; they are showing that they have run out of actual arguments. They cannot debate the policy, so they try to intimidate the person. It is a tired strategy that remains the default setting for those who mistake fear for influence.
The Cost of Not Selling Out
We have seen this script before. The phone calls, the visits, the vague warnings about future troubles—it is all designed to make the target feel isolated. But the irony is that these tactics usually have the opposite effect. They clarify the choice. You either stand for something that actually matters, or you trade your spine for a seat at a table that is already collapsing. We aren't interested in their seats, and we certainly aren't interested in the consequences they think they can manufacture.
If you are tired of the constant threats and the hollow politics of coercion, consider where you stand. You can continue watching the circus from the sidelines, or you can head over to /join to see how we are building something different. We aren't here to play the game; we are here to change the rules entirely. Take a moment to read our /manifesto, and decide if you want to keep being a spectator or if you are ready to be part of the nuisance that actually makes a difference.
